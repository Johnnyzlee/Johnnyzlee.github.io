Quantitative Research Interview Mathematics Review

← Blogs · updated 2026-07-20
中文English

This is a high-coverage mathematics checklist for quantitative research written tests and interviews. It is designed for rapid recall, not as a first textbook. Standard terms are kept explicit because interviews often expect them directly.

Priority: P0 = must be able to derive and use without notes; P1 = commonly tested; P2 = role-dependent bonus. For every checkbox, require yourself to: define it, state the assumptions, derive the key formula, solve one small example, and explain one failure mode.

0. High-priority review order

Review in this order:

  1. P0 regression: derive OLS; diagnose multicollinearity; compare deletion, PCA/SVD, ridge and lasso; explain heteroskedasticity and leakage.

  2. P0 probability/statistics: conditional probability, expectation, variance/covariance, common distributions, estimation, confidence intervals and hypothesis tests.

  3. P0 ML: bias-variance, regularization, validation, linear/logistic models, trees and ensembles, PCA, evaluation metrics.

  4. P0 counting: permutations/combinations, recurrence, state design and dynamic programming.

  5. P0 linear algebra: rank, projections, eigenvalues, SVD, positive semidefinite covariance matrices and conditioning.

  6. P1 time series and quant research: returns, stationarity, autocorrelation, rolling features, time-aware validation, transaction costs and multiple testing.

1. Probability

1.1 Events, counting and probability rules [P0]

  • □ Sample space, event, complement, union, intersection, mutually exclusive events.

  • □ Axioms: $P(A)\ge0$, $P(\Omega)=1$, and additivity for disjoint events.

  • □ Complement and inclusion-exclusion: $P(A\cup B)=P(A)\mathplus{}P(B)-P(A\cap B)$.

  • □ Equally likely outcomes require counting the correct atomic outcomes; sums of two dice are not equally likely.

  • □ Multiplication rule, permutations, combinations and multinomial coefficients.

  • □ Sampling with/without replacement; ordered/unordered; distinguishability; circular arrangements.

  • □ Inclusion-exclusion, stars and bars, pigeonhole principle, symmetry and complementary counting.

Interview reflex: before calculating, say what one outcome is and why outcomes are equally likely. Most counting errors come from changing the sample space halfway through.

1.2 Conditional probability, independence and Bayes [P0]

  • □ Conditional probability: $P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}$, for $P(B)>0$.

  • □ Product rule: $P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)P(B)$.

  • □ Law of total probability over a partition {B_i}.

  • □ Bayes rule: posterior is proportional to likelihood times prior.

  • □ Pairwise independence is weaker than mutual independence.

  • □ Conditional independence can hold when marginal independence does not, and vice versa.

  • □ Base-rate neglect, prosecutor's fallacy, Monty Hall and false-positive calculations.

Quick check: if a disease prevalence is 1%, sensitivity is 99%, and specificity is 95%, can you compute P(disease | positive) without confusing P(A | B) with P(B | A)?

1.3 Random variables and distributions [P0]

  • □ PMF, PDF, CDF, survival function; discrete versus continuous variables.

  • □ Joint, marginal and conditional distributions; independence via factorization.

  • □ Transformation of variables; Jacobian for continuous variables; convolution for sums.

  • □ LOTUS: compute E[g(X)] from the distribution of X without first finding the distribution of g(X).

  • □ Quantiles, median, mode, skewness, kurtosis and why moments may not exist.

Discrete families:

  • □ $\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p)$: mean $p$, variance $p(1-p)$.

  • □ $\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p)$: independent Bernoulli count; mean $np$, variance $np(1-p)$.

  • □ Geometric(p): waiting time, memoryless; be explicit whether support starts at 0 or 1.

  • □ Negative binomial: trials/failures before the r-th success; state the convention.

  • □ Hypergeometric: sampling without replacement; finite-population correction.

  • □ $\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$: count in a fixed interval; sum of independent Poissons; binomial rare-event approximation.

  • □ Multinomial and occupancy problems.

Continuous families:

  • □ Uniform(a,b): location-scale and geometric probability.

  • □ $\operatorname{Exponential}(\lambda)$: waiting time and memorylessness; minimum of independent exponentials.

  • □ Gamma/Erlang: waiting time to multiple Poisson events; beta-gamma relationships.

  • □ Normal and standard normal: affine transformations, sums, standardization.

  • □ Multivariate normal: mean vector, covariance matrix, conditional Gaussian, zero covariance implies independence only in special families such as jointly Gaussian variables.

  • □ Lognormal: positive multiplicative quantities; arithmetic versus geometric return.

  • □ Beta and Dirichlet: probabilities/proportions and conjugate priors.

  • □ Chi-square, Student t and F: sampling distributions behind variance estimates and classical tests.

  • □ Pareto/heavy tails: tail risk, unstable high moments and why Gaussian intuition can fail for returns.

1.4 Expectation, variance and covariance [P0]

  • □ Linearity: $\mathbb E(aX\mathplus{}bY\mathplus{}c)=a\mathbb E X\mathplus{}b\mathbb E Y\mathplus{}c$, with no independence assumption.

  • □ Indicator method: $\mathbb E(\sum_i I_i)=\sum_iP(I_i=1)$; use it for matches, collisions, inversions and occupancy.

  • □ Tail-sum formula for nonnegative integer $X$: $\mathbb E X=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}P(X\ge k)$.

  • □ $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E(X^2)-(\mathbb E X)^2$; $\operatorname{Var}(aX\mathplus{}b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)$.

  • □ $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\mathbb E(XY)-\mathbb E X\,\mathbb E Y$; correlation is scale-free but not a measure of general dependence.

  • □ $\operatorname{Var}(\sum_iX_i)=\sum_i\operatorname{Var}(X_i)\mathplus{}2\sum_{i<j}\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)$.

  • □ Independence implies zero covariance when moments exist; the converse is false in general.

  • □ Law of total expectation: $\mathbb E X=\mathbb E(\mathbb E(X\mid Y))$.

  • □ Law of total variance: $\operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E(\operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y))\mathplus{}\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E(X\mid Y))$.

  • □ Conditional covariance and covariance matrices.

Interview reflex: try indicators, symmetry, conditioning, recursion, or a state equation before brute-force enumeration.

1.5 Order statistics and geometric probability [P1]

  • □ For i.i.d. samples with CDF $F$, $P(\max_iX_i\le x)=F(x)^n$ and $P(\min_iX_i>x)=(1-F(x))^n$.

  • □ Density of the $k$-th order statistic and $\mathbb E U_{(k)}=\frac{k}{n\mathplus{}1}$ for uniform order statistics.

  • □ Range, spacings and broken-stick/simplex arguments.

  • □ Random points in intervals, rectangles, circles and triangles; draw the feasible region before integrating.

  • □ Exchangeability and symmetry shortcuts.

1.6 Limits, inequalities and approximations [P1]

  • □ Markov, Chebyshev, Jensen, union bound and Cauchy-Schwarz.

  • □ Weak/strong law of large numbers: sample average convergence and assumptions.

  • □ Central limit theorem; standard error scales as $1\mathord/\sqrt n$; know when dependence/heavy tails break naive use.

  • □ Normal, Poisson and continuity-corrected approximations to binomial probabilities.

  • □ Delta method, Slutsky's theorem and continuous mapping theorem at an interview-level intuition.

  • □ Chernoff/Hoeffding concentration bounds [P2].

1.7 Stochastic processes [P1/P2]

  • □ Discrete-time Markov chains: transition matrix, n-step transitions, communicating classes, recurrence, absorption and stationary distribution.

  • □ First-step analysis for hitting probabilities and expected hitting times.

  • □ Random walks, gambler's ruin and reflection/symmetry arguments.

  • □ Poisson process: independent stationary increments, exponential interarrival times, thinning and superposition.

  • □ Martingale definition, fair-game intuition and when optional stopping is invalid.

  • □ Brownian motion, quadratic variation and Ito intuition [P2, mainly derivatives/systematic macro roles].

2. Statistics

2.1 Samples, estimators and sampling distributions [P0]

  • □ Population versus sample; statistic versus parameter; i.i.d. assumptions.

  • □ Sample mean, unbiased sample variance, covariance and correlation.

  • □ Bias, variance, MSE = variance + bias², consistency, efficiency and sufficiency.

  • □ Standard error is the standard deviation of an estimator, not the sample standard deviation.

  • □ Bootstrap: resample observations/blocks and know when dependence requires block bootstrap.

  • □ Jackknife and permutation/randomization tests at a conceptual level.

  • □ Robust summaries: median, MAD, trimmed/winsorized mean; sensitivity to outliers.

2.2 Estimation: MLE, MAP and Bayesian updating [P0]

  • □ Write likelihood and log-likelihood; differentiate; verify boundary and second-order conditions.

  • □ MLE invariance, asymptotic normality and Fisher information.

  • □ Method of moments and comparison with MLE.

  • □ MAP = likelihood plus log-prior; distinguish MAP from posterior mean.

  • □ Conjugate pairs: beta-binomial, gamma-Poisson/exponential, normal-normal.

  • □ L2 regularization corresponds to a Gaussian prior; L1 corresponds to a Laplace prior under the usual MAP interpretation.

2.3 Confidence intervals and hypothesis tests [P0]

  • □ A 95% frequentist confidence interval is a procedure with 95% long-run coverage, not a 95% posterior probability statement about a fixed parameter.

  • □ Null/alternative, test statistic, rejection region, p-value, significance level.

  • □ Type I/II errors, power and sample-size trade-offs.

  • □ One/two-sample z and t tests; paired tests; equal versus unequal variance (Welch).

  • □ Chi-square goodness-of-fit/independence; F tests and ANOVA assumptions.

  • □ One-sided versus two-sided tests; pre-specification matters.

  • □ Multiple testing: Bonferroni, family-wise error, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR, data snooping.

  • □ Statistical significance versus economic significance; effect size and confidence interval.

2.4 Linear regression [P0]

Model: $y=X\beta+\varepsilon$. If $X$ has full column rank, OLS minimizes $\lVert y-X\beta\rVert_2^2$ and solves $X^{\mathsf T}X\hat\beta=X^{\mathsf T}y$, giving

$$ \hat\beta=(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}y. $$

Numerically, prefer QR/SVD to explicitly computing an inverse.

  • □ Derive OLS by gradient and by orthogonal projection.

  • □ Interpret coefficients conditionally; distinguish prediction from causal interpretation.

  • □ Gauss-Markov assumptions and BLUE; normal errors are needed for exact small-sample t/F inference, not for the OLS point estimate.

  • □ Residuals, fitted values, leverage, hat matrix $H=X(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}$, influence and Cook's distance.

  • □ R² versus adjusted R²; training R² never decreases when adding features.

  • □ Heteroskedasticity: coefficients may remain unbiased under exogeneity, but classical standard errors fail; use robust/sandwich SE where appropriate.

  • □ Autocorrelation and clustered dependence; HAC/Newey-West or clustered SE conceptually.

  • □ Endogeneity/omitted variables and why correlation does not identify causality.

Multicollinearity: predictors are nearly linearly dependent. OLS predictions can remain reasonable while individual coefficients become unstable and standard errors inflate. Diagnose with correlation structure, small singular values/large condition number, or VIF. Remedies depend on the goal:

  • remove/merge redundant features using domain knowledge;

  • PCA/SVD or other dimension reduction;

  • ridge for stable dense coefficients; lasso/elastic net for sparsity and selection;

  • collect more informative data;

  • never claim that adding interaction terms generally fixes collinearity—they can increase it unless motivated and centered.

2.5 Regularization and model selection [P0]

  • □ Ridge: minimize $\mathrm{SSE}\mathplus{}\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert_2^2$; shrinks correlated coefficients, rarely exactly to zero.

  • □ Lasso: minimize $\mathrm{SSE}\mathplus{}\lambda\lVert\beta\rVert_1$; creates sparsity but is unstable among strongly correlated predictors.

  • □ Elastic net combines L1/L2; useful for groups of correlated features.

  • □ Standardize features before scale-sensitive penalties; usually do not penalize the intercept.

  • □ Choose λ using validation within a leakage-safe pipeline.

  • □ AIC/BIC, likelihood penalties and cross-validation; know their different goals and assumptions.

2.6 Time series and financial data [P0/P1]

  • □ Simple return $r_t=\frac{P_t}{P_{t-1}}-1$; log return $g_t=\log P_t-\log P_{t-1}$; log returns add across time.

  • □ Arithmetic mean return, geometric growth and volatility drag.

  • □ Stationarity, trend, seasonality, structural breaks and regime change.

  • □ Autocovariance, ACF/PACF; white noise versus martingale difference.

  • □ AR, MA, ARMA/ARIMA: assumptions, stationarity/invertibility and forecasting intuition.

  • □ Unit roots, spurious regression, differencing and cointegration [P1].

  • □ Volatility clustering, ARCH/GARCH intuition [P1].

  • □ Cross-sectional versus time-series dependence; panel/fixed-effects intuition.

  • □ Sharpe ratio and annualization assumptions; serial correlation invalidates naive sqrt(time) scaling.

  • □ Drawdown, turnover, capacity, slippage, transaction cost and look-ahead/survivorship bias.

3. Linear algebra

3.1 Vectors, spaces, rank and systems [P0]

  • □ Vector norms L1/L2/L-infinity; inner product, angle and cosine similarity.

  • □ Linear combination, span, independence, basis and dimension.

  • □ Column space, row space, null space and left null space; rank-nullity.

  • □ Solve Ax=b by elimination; zero/one/infinite solutions; consistency means b is in Col(A).

  • □ Rank properties: $\operatorname{rank}(AB)\le\min(\operatorname{rank}(A),\operatorname{rank}(B))$ and $\operatorname{rank}(A)=\operatorname{rank}(A^{\mathsf T})$.

  • □ Invertibility equivalences for a square matrix: full rank, trivial null space, nonzero determinant, no zero eigenvalue.

  • □ Determinant as signed volume scaling; effects of row operations; $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$.

3.2 Orthogonality, projection and least squares [P0]

  • □ Orthogonal/orthonormal vectors; orthogonal matrix $Q$ has $Q^{\mathsf T}Q=I$ and preserves lengths.

  • □ Projection onto $\operatorname{span}(A)$: $P=A(A^{\mathsf T}A)^{-1}A^{\mathsf T}$ when columns are independent.

  • □ Least-squares residual is orthogonal to $\operatorname{Col}(A)$: $A^{\mathsf T}(y-A\hat\beta)=0$.

  • □ Gram-Schmidt and QR; QR solves least squares more stably than normal equations.

  • □ Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse and minimum-norm solutions.

3.3 Eigenvalues, PSD matrices and dynamics [P0]

  • □ $Av=\lambda v$; trace is the sum and determinant is the product of eigenvalues, with multiplicity.

  • □ Diagonalization $A=V\Lambda V^{-1}$; powers and linear recurrences.

  • □ Real symmetric matrices have real eigenvalues and orthonormal eigenvectors: $A=Q\Lambda Q^{\mathsf T}$.

  • □ Positive semidefinite (PSD): $x^{\mathsf T}Ax\ge0$; covariance and Gram matrices are PSD.

  • □ Positive definite tests: positive eigenvalues, Cholesky factorization, leading principal minors (for symmetric matrices).

  • □ Rayleigh quotient and largest/smallest eigenvalues.

  • □ Markov transition matrices and stationary eigenvectors; be explicit about row- versus column-stochastic convention.

3.4 Matrix decompositions [P0/P1]

  • □ LU: elimination and repeated solves.

  • □ QR: orthogonalization and least squares.

  • □ Eigendecomposition: square diagonalizable operators.

  • □ SVD: $A=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}$; works for any rectangular matrix; rank, pseudoinverse, best low-rank approximation and condition number.

  • □ PCA: center X, eigendecompose covariance or use SVD; directions maximize variance; scores versus loadings; scaling matters.

  • □ Cholesky: fast factorization of symmetric positive definite matrices; Gaussian simulation and covariance solves.

3.5 Matrix calculus and optimization prerequisites [P0]

  • □ Shapes first: scalar-by-vector gradient, Jacobian and Hessian.

  • □ $\nabla_x(a^{\mathsf T}x)=a$ and $\nabla_x(x^{\mathsf T}Ax)=(A\mathplus{}A^{\mathsf T})x$; for symmetric $A$ this is $2Ax$.

  • □ $\nabla_x\lVert Ax-b\rVert_2^2=2A^{\mathsf T}(Ax-b)$.

  • □ ∇_X tr(A'X)=A; cyclic trace trick; d log det X = tr(X^(-1)dX).

  • □ Chain rule/backpropagation through vector functions.

  • □ Gradient, Hessian, critical point, convexity and strong convexity.

  • □ Lagrange multipliers and KKT conditions (feasibility, stationarity, complementary slackness).

  • □ Gradient descent convergence intuition; learning rate; SGD noise; momentum and adaptive methods.

3.6 Numerical linear algebra [P1]

  • □ Never compute an inverse merely to solve Ax=b; use a solve/factorization.

  • □ Condition number measures sensitivity; X'X squares the condition number of X.

  • □ Floating-point precision, cancellation, overflow/underflow and log-sum-exp.

  • □ Sherman-Morrison and Woodbury identities for low-rank updates [P2].

  • □ Sparse matrices and iterative methods; power iteration/conjugate gradient intuition [P2].

4. Combinatorics and discrete problem solving

4.1 Counting toolkit [P0]

  • □ Sum/product rules; permutations, combinations, multinomial coefficients.

  • □ Ordered versus unordered selections; repetition allowed or not.

  • □ Binomial theorem and identities such as $\binom nk=\binom{n-1}{k}\mathplus{}\binom{n-1}{k-1}$.

  • □ Stars and bars with upper/lower-bound corrections.

  • □ Inclusion-exclusion and derangements.

  • □ Pigeonhole principle, double counting and bijective proofs.

  • □ Circular permutations and quotienting by rotations/reflections.

4.2 Recurrences, generating functions and DP [P0/P1]

  • □ Define a state that contains exactly the information needed for the future.

  • □ Base cases, transition, evaluation order and final aggregation.

  • □ Memoization versus tabulation; time/space complexity; rolling-array optimization.

  • □ Linear recurrences, characteristic equations and matrix exponentiation.

  • □ Ordinary generating functions as algebraic encodings of sequences [P1].

  • □ Catalan structures: balanced parentheses, binary trees and noncrossing matchings [P1].

  • □ Stirling numbers and Bell numbers for partitions [P2].

4.3 Graphs, invariants and constructive reasoning [P1]

  • □ Graph, degree, path, cycle, connected component, tree and bipartite graph.

  • □ Handshaking lemma; tree has n-1 edges; unique path characterization.

  • □ Euler trails versus Hamiltonian paths; do not confuse their criteria.

  • □ Coloring, matching and shortest paths at a conceptual level.

  • □ Invariants, monovariants, parity, extremal principle and symmetry.

  • □ Probabilistic method: prove existence by showing positive probability [P2].

Triangle-walk pattern: encode the current vertex/direction or use adjacency-matrix powers. Ask whether the walk is on three vertices, a triangular lattice, or a continuous geometric triangle—the wording changes the state space completely.

5. Machine-learning mathematics

5.1 Problem setup and generalization [P0]

  • □ Supervised/unsupervised learning; regression/classification/ranking.

  • □ Empirical risk minimization: average loss plus regularization.

  • □ Train/validation/test roles; hyperparameters must not be chosen on test data.

  • □ Bias-variance trade-off; irreducible noise; underfit versus overfit diagnostics.

  • □ Parametric versus nonparametric; generative versus discriminative.

  • □ Curse of dimensionality and why distance concentration hurts nearest-neighbor methods.

  • □ Data leakage, selection bias, distribution shift and nonstationarity.

5.2 Linear and generalized linear models [P0]

  • □ Linear regression, weighted least squares, ridge/lasso/elastic net.

  • □ Logistic regression: sigmoid, log-odds, Bernoulli likelihood, cross-entropy and gradient.

  • □ Softmax regression and numerical stabilization.

  • □ GLM components: random component, linear predictor and link function.

  • □ Interactions and nonlinear basis expansion; regularize and validate.

  • □ Calibration versus discrimination.

5.3 Trees and ensembles [P0]

  • □ Regression/classification trees; greedy split; MSE, entropy and Gini impurity.

  • □ Depth/min-leaf/pruning and the bias-variance effect.

  • □ Bagging and random forests: bootstrap plus feature subsampling reduces correlation/variance.

  • □ Boosting: sequentially fit residuals/negative gradients; learning rate and number of trees trade off.

  • □ Feature importance pitfalls: impurity importance bias and correlated predictors; use permutation importance carefully.

5.4 SVM, kernels and nearest neighbors [P1]

  • □ Maximum-margin classifier; soft margin and slack; role of C.

  • □ Hinge loss; support vectors; primal/dual intuition.

  • □ Kernel trick and valid PSD kernels; RBF bandwidth.

  • □ k-NN bias/variance, scaling and distance metrics.

5.5 Probabilistic and unsupervised models [P1]

  • □ Naive Bayes and conditional-independence assumption; Laplace smoothing.

  • □ Gaussian discriminant analysis: LDA versus QDA covariance assumptions.

  • □ k-means objective, Lloyd iterations, initialization and local minima.

  • □ GMM and EM: E-step responsibilities, M-step weighted MLE; likelihood is nondecreasing but local optimum/singularity issues remain.

  • □ PCA versus factor models versus ICA; reconstruction and interpretation.

  • □ Anomaly detection and density-estimation basics.

5.6 Neural networks [P1; P0 for AI-quant roles]

  • □ Affine layer, activation, depth/width and universal approximation intuition.

  • □ Backpropagation as repeated chain rule; derive gradients for a two-layer network.

  • □ ReLU, sigmoid, tanh; saturation and vanishing/exploding gradients.

  • □ Initialization, normalization, dropout, weight decay and early stopping.

  • □ CNN: locality and weight sharing; RNN/LSTM/GRU: recurrence and gating; Transformer: scaled dot-product attention and positional information.

  • □ Batch normalization training versus inference behavior.

  • □ Why a larger model can generalize: optimization/implicit regularization intuition, not simply parameter count.

5.7 Metrics and validation [P0]

  • □ Regression: MSE/RMSE, MAE, Huber, R²; sensitivity to scale/outliers.

  • □ Classification: confusion matrix, precision, recall, specificity, F1, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC.

  • □ Log loss/Brier score and probability calibration.

  • □ Class imbalance, threshold choice and cost-sensitive evaluation.

  • □ k-fold, stratified and grouped CV; nested CV for honest tuning.

  • □ Time series: expanding/rolling walk-forward splits, embargo/purging where labels overlap; never randomly shuffle future into past.

  • □ Fit preprocessing only on each training fold; use a pipeline.

5.8 Quant-research-specific model judgment [P0]

  • □ Define the target at prediction time and prove every feature is available then.

  • □ Cross-sectional IC/rank IC versus time-series prediction metrics.

  • □ Neutralization/exposure control, universe selection and missing-data semantics.

  • □ Backtest net of fees, spread, slippage, market impact and turnover.

  • □ Survivorship, look-ahead, stale prices, corporate actions and delisting bias.

  • □ Multiple factors/hyperparameters create multiple-testing and selection bias; keep a true holdout and report uncertainty.

  • □ Regime stability, rolling diagnostics and stress tests matter more than one aggregate score.

  • □ A model with higher offline R² can have worse PnL because ranking, tails, capacity and costs differ.

6. One-page formula recall

$$ P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E(X^2)-(\mathbb E X)^2. $$
$$ \operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E(\operatorname{Var}(X\mid Y)) +\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb E(X\mid Y)). $$
$$ \hat\beta_{\mathrm{OLS}}=(X^{\mathsf T}X)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}y, \qquad \hat\beta_{\mathrm{ridge}}=(X^{\mathsf T}X+\lambda I)^{-1}X^{\mathsf T}y. $$
$$ P=A(A^{\mathsf T}A)^{-1}A^{\mathsf T}, \qquad A=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}. $$
$$ p=\frac{1}{1+\exp(-x^{\mathsf T}\beta)}, \qquad \nabla L=X^{\mathsf T}(p-y). $$
$$ \operatorname{Precision}=\frac{TP}{TP+FP}, \quad \operatorname{Recall}=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}, \quad F_1=\frac{2PR}{P+R}. $$

7. How to perform the knowledge check

  1. Closed-book pass: mark every checkbox Green (derive/explain), Yellow (recognize only), or Red (cannot recall).

  2. Derivation pass: without notes, derive Bayes, total variance, OLS, ridge, logistic gradient, PCA/SVD connection and one DP recurrence.

  3. Oral pass: explain each P0 item in 60–90 seconds with assumptions and one counterexample.

  4. Problem pass: solve 10 probability, 5 statistics, 5 linear algebra, 5 combinatorics and 5 ML questions under time pressure.

  5. Coding pass: simulate one probability result, implement OLS/logistic loss, merge intervals and one DP problem in plain Python.

  6. Error log: record the trigger you missed, the wrong assumption, the correct pattern and a re-test date.

8. Sources and next reading

This checklist synthesizes recurring interview themes rather than reproducing proprietary tests. Start with these sources:

  • Jane Street Probability & Markets Guide — official probability, expectation, conditional probability and interview-solving primer.

  • Jane Street Probability Playing Cards — official graded probability practice; Jane Street recommends the early clubs/diamonds for interview preparation.

  • Harvard Stat 110 Strategic Practice — topic-organized probability problems with solutions.

  • MIT 18.06 Linear Algebra — systems, subspaces, least squares, eigenvalues, PSD matrices and SVD.

  • Stanford CS229 and An Introduction to Statistical Learning — ML theory, models, validation and freely available official materials.

  • Xinfeng Zhou, A Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance Interviews (the Green Book); Timothy Crack, Heard on the Street; Frederick Mosteller, Fifty Challenging Problems in Probability — use legitimate copies and solve actively rather than memorizing wording.

Scope note: derivatives pricing, stochastic calculus, C++ systems and market microstructure can dominate some specialized roles, but the requested focus here is quantitative research probability/statistics, linear algebra, combinatorics and ML. Add those modules when a job description explicitly requires them.
